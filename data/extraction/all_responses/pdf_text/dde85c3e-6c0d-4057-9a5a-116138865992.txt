    Two additional series of marked branches were experi-
mentally damaged. In the first series (n = 22), each branch
received a small, 1-cm 2 circular tissue lesion on the upper
side of the branch at a distance of 4 to 9 cm from the tip.
Lesions were made with compressed air (method: Bak
et al., 1977). In the second series (n = 10), I inflicted ad-
ditional damage by scraping protruding skeletal elements,
such as corallites, off the lesions. The resulting tissue +
skeleton lesions were in all other aspects similar to the
tissue lesions. The first series was made on 6 September
 1977. Growth measurements were taken on 9 September
 1977 and 4 November 1977. The second series of tissue +
skeleton lesions was made on 15 September 1977. Growth
measurements were taken on this date and on 4 Novem-
ber 1977. All lesions were observed and measured at
weekly or biweekly intervals.
    To obtain information on the mode of regeneration,
possible inclusion of foreign material and the structure of
the regenerated skeleton, samples were collected of normal
branch surfaces ( n = 11) and of both types of lesions
(n= 10) after complete regeneration for thin sectioning
and X-ray diffraction.

  inclusions or any traces of a cause of abnormal calcifica-

 a coenosteal framework of vesicular dissepiments (Fig. 2A),
 similar to normal exothecal coenosteum.
