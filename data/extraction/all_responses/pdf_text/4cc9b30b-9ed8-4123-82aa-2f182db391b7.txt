                                                                        Discussion

                                                                        This study indicates that experimental lesions, simulating
                                                                        A. meleagris bites, on P. compressa heal in a little over a
                                                                        month, and with little effect on the long-term growth of
                                                                        even very small coral fragments (1 cm).
                                                                           Fast and complete regeneration of tissue across
                                                                        P. compressa is likely because of the small size of the bite
                                                                        lesions. A. meleagris feeds on the branch tips of the finger-
                                                                        like P. compressa, and therefore inflicted lesions will tend
                                                                        to be circular in shape and similarly limited in size (to the
                                                                        size of coral branches). Rapid healing of lesions may also
                                                                        be explained by polyps at the tip of branches being younger
                                                                        and more active than polyps at the base, as documented for
