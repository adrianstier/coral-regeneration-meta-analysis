   On Hawaiian shallow reefs it is common to see lesions
caused by fish bites on the live tissue of coral colonies
where the bare skeleton is exposed. While there is a general
perception that parrotfishes are responsible for these
lesions, less than 2% of parrotfish bites are inflicted on live
coral, and these are generally along the edge where the
coral tissue meets the algae (pers. obs., Ong 2007). The
majority of bite lesions in the Main Hawaiian Islands are
inflicted by the barred filefish (Cantherhines dumerilii) and
the spotted puffer (Arothron meleagris), which have been
observed by the authors to remove coral skeleton and inflict
